SYNTHETIC EXPERIENCE

1. Anchor retrosynthetic disconnections on readily available, pre-functionalized commercial building blocks and catalog heterocycles, avoiding unnecessary de novo core synthesis or step-inflating preparation from elementary chemical feedstocks.
2. Deploy protecting groups pragmatically: protect polyfunctional amines, phenols, and polyols early when competitive cross-reactivity or catalyst poisoning is a risk, avoiding both precarious unprotected reactions and redundant protection–deprotection loops.
3. Evaluate chemoselectivity and functional group compatibility pragmatically across downstream steps, avoiding overly defensive deferrals of heterocyclic moieties that force inefficient, non-convergent linear linkages when standard coupling conditions tolerate them.
4. Establish stereocenters efficiently using chiral-pool precursors, biocatalysis, or asymmetric catalysis, leveraging reliable late-stage catalytic asymmetric transformations (such as asymmetric transfer hydrogenations or stereoselective ketone reductions) when installing chiral centers too early forces delicate functionality through long, unnecessary sequences.
5. Prioritize the procurement of pre-assembled, commercially functionalized heterocyclic and bicyclic cores over lengthy de novo multi-step annulation cascades, reserving de novo ring construction strictly for substitution patterns unattainable via catalog precursors.
6. Sequence cross-couplings on polyhalogenated heteroarenes by leveraging innate electronic and steric differentiation under mild conditions before applying catalyst/ligand control or directing groups.
7. Generate hazardous, toxic, or thermally labile intermediates in situ or telescope them without isolation, utilizing controlled addition and low steady-state concentrations to mitigate runaway risks.
8. Maximize redox economy by selecting starting materials that match the core's required oxidation state, strictly avoiding circuitous multi-step redox cascades (such as acid-to-alcohol-to-halide progressions) in favor of direct functionalizations or appropriately oxidized commercial precursors.
9. Perform mono-functionalization of symmetric bifunctional substrates under kinetic or statistical control only if the mono-derivatized intermediate cleanly separates from unreacted and bis-substituted material; otherwise, employ desymmetrization or pre-differentiated precursors.
10. Telescope compatible consecutive steps without intermediate workups to maximize throughput, while deliberately scheduling discrete isolations at crystalline stages to purge accumulated process impurities.
11. Mask basic nitrogens and strongly coordinating heteroatoms with appropriate non-nucleophilic precursors (such as azides, nitro groups, or carbamates) only when competitive cross-reactivity or severe catalyst deactivation is substantiated, avoiding unnecessary late-stage functionalization bottlenecks.
