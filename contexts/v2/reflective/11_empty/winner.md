SYNTHETIC EXPERIENCE

1. Balance convergent fragment assembly against overall route viability, avoiding premature dense functionalization that compromises downstream step feasibility.
2. Select between *de novo* ring annulation and core functionalization based on regiocontrol, using symmetrical precursors or classical cyclizations to avoid ambiguous regiochemical mixtures.
3. Depend on robust classical aromatic substitutions, halogenations, and directed metalations, reserving direct C–H functionalizations for positions with unambiguous innate reactivity.
4. Differentiate polyhalogenated heteroaryls by exploiting positional reactivity hierarchies through staged SNAr and transition-metal-catalyzed cross-couplings.
5. Restrict SNAr disconnections to electron-deficient heteroaryl halides, employing transition-metal cross-couplings for deactivated, neutral, or sterically hindered positions.
6. Exploit intrinsic chemoselectivity where margins are robust, but deploy strategic transient protection when differentiating electronically or structurally similar nucleophilic or acidic sites.
7. Exploit relative nucleophilicity and electrophilicity differentials in polyfunctional motifs through staged, chemoselective operations.
8. Defer oxidation-sensitive motifs, basic amines, and delicate handles to late stages to insulate them from harsh upstream reagents.
9. Prioritize robust, thermodynamically favored ring closures over strained or entropically disfavored cyclizations.
10. Sequence transformations from harshest to mildest, introducing sensitive stereocenters and labile linkages as late as feasible.
